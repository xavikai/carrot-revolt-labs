One texture, a whole castle.

A trim sheet is a texture made of horizontal strips that repeat along U: planks, stone courses, beams, moldings, iron straps and bevels. Read one, plan one by texel density, put UV islands on its strips with G, S and R as in Blender, round edges with a bevel strip and Weighted Normal, and texture many props with a single material.

    Shortcuts you can use here

    Keys work while the mouse is over the workspace. Transforms act on the selected UV islands, as in the UV Editor.

    LMB · ShiftLMB

    A · AltA
    Select all · select none
    G · S · R

    X · Y
    Lock the move or the scale to U or V
    Ctrl (held)
    Snap: 8 px steps, 5° steps, 0.1 scale steps
    LMB / Enter · RMB / Esc
    Confirm · cancel
    Home · Wheel · MMB

    BACK IN BLENDER

    Working with trim sheets in Blender

    Blender has no special trim-sheet mode: it is ordinary UV editing with a plan. These are the tools used in this lab.

    1. Open the sheet in the UV Editor (Image › Open) and assign it to one material shared by all the props.
    2. In Edit Mode, select a band of faces and unwrap it with UV › Follow Active Quads (U in the 3D Viewport) so it comes out straight. UV › Align Rotation turns it to the axes.
    3. Move, scale and rotate islands with G S R; X/Y lock an axis and Ctrl snaps. Scale uniformly to keep one texel density; add-ons such as Texel Density Checker measure and set it.
    4. Islands can overlap and run past the edges of the image in U: the texture repeats. Keep them inside their strip in V.
    5. For round edges: Bevel modifier (Width Type: Width, Segments 1), map the chamfer faces to a bevel strip, then Shade Smooth and a Weighted Normal modifier (Face Area).
    6. Trim-sheet add-ons add a fit to trim operator that does the scaling and placing in one click.
