    SAME KEYS AS BLENDER

    Shortcuts you can use here

    Keys work in the editor under the mouse, just like in Blender: the same G moves a control in the 3D Viewport, keys in the Graph Editor and keys in time in the Timeline.

    LMB · 3D
    Select a rig control (Root, SS_Top, SS_Bottom). G moves it: the Root in X and Z (X / Z lock an axis), the squash & stretch controls up or down. Type a number for an exact value. I inserts a keyframe, AltG clears it
    LMB · Timeline / Dope Sheet
    Drag the numbers to change frame · click a keyframe to select it (Shift adds; on the Summary or a bone row it takes every channel) · drag it or press G to move it in time · ShiftD duplicates · drag on empty space to box-select · X deletes. Switch the editor with its Editor Type menu.
    LMB · Graph
    Select a keyframe or handle · Shift to add · drag to move · drag on empty space to box-select
    G · X / Y
    Move the selected keys; X only in time, Y only in value. Click or Enter to confirm, Esc to cancel.
    A · AltA
    Select all / none
    I
    Insert a keyframe on the active channel at the current frame
    X · Delete
    Delete the selected keyframes
    T
    Interpolation Mode: Constant, Linear, Bezier
    V
    Handle Type: Free, Aligned, Vector, Automatic, Auto Clamped
    Home · Numpad .

    BACK IN BLENDER

    The same bounce in Blender

    This lab shows a simplified Blender: one object, a side camera and a few channels. The curves, handles and keys behave like Blender's F-curves.

    1. Append the ball rig: a Root control at the base and two squash & stretch controls, SS_Top and SS_Bottom. Select it and switch to Pose Mode.
    2. Open the Animation workspace. Pose the Root at frame 1 with G and key it with I; key the contacts and the tops. Move keys in time in the Timeline or the Dope Sheet.
    3. In the Graph Editor, select all and press T › Bezier; select the contacts and press V › Vector.
    4. Make every bounce lower and shorter. Turn on Motion Paths (Object properties) to see the spacing.
    5. Use the rig's squash & stretch controls: the top one at the contacts (pivot at the base), the bottom one to stretch towards the floor. The rig keeps the volume.
    6. Shape weight with the handles: long at the top for light objects, short and sharp for heavy ones.
