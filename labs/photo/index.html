    KEY IDEAS

    One stop = double or half the light

    Each of the three settings changes the light and something else. Balancing them is the whole craft of exposure.

    Aperture · f/
    How wide the lens opens. f/2 lets in 4× more light than f/4. A wide aperture gives a shallow depth of field.
    Shutter speed
    How long the sensor is exposed. 1/60 lets in twice the light of 1/125. Slow speeds blur movement and camera shake.
    ISO
    How much the signal is amplified. ISO 800 is twice as bright as ISO 400, with more noise.
    Focal length
    The angle of view: 16 mm is wide, 200 mm is narrow. Perspective depends on the distance, not on the lens.
    Sensor size
    A smaller sensor crops the image: APS-C × 1.5, Micro Four Thirds × 2 (equivalent focal length).
    Modes
    M: you choose everything. Av (A): you choose the aperture. Tv (S): you choose the shutter speed. The camera fills in the rest.
    BACK IN BLENDER

    The same camera in Blender

    Blender's camera is a pinhole with a lens: it has focal length, sensor, focus and aperture, and a shutter for motion blur. It has no ISO and no automatic exposure: brightness is set with the lights and Color Management › Exposure.

    1. Camera Properties › Lens › Focal Length (mm). Lens Unit can show it as Field of View instead.
    2. Camera › Sensor Fit and Size: 36 mm is full frame. Enter the real sensor width to match a real camera.
    3. Depth of Field: turn it on, pick a Focus Object or a Focus Distance, and set the F-Stop. Blades shape the bokeh.
    4. Render Properties › Motion Blur › Shutter is in frames: seconds = Shutter ÷ frame rate. 0.5 is a 180° shutter.
    5. In Cycles, DoF and motion blur come from sampling the aperture and the exposure time, as in this lab.
    6. Match the real Frame Rate (Output Properties) when you put 3D objects into filmed footage.
