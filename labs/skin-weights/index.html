Paint the weights. Pose to check.

Each vertex follows the bones in proportion to its weights. Paint them in Weight Paint mode as in Blender, bend the bones and fix what breaks: the elbow, the zero weights, the symmetry and the limits of a game engine.

    BACK IN BLENDER

    A good skin, step by step

    This lab shows a simplified Blender: tube-shaped meshes, bones that bend and twist, and the tools that matter most. The weights, the colours and the operators behave like Blender's.

    1. Name the bones with .L and .R and apply the scale of mesh and armature (Ctrl A › All Transforms) before skinning.
    2. Select the mesh, then the armature, and Ctrl P › With Automatic Weights. It is a good start, never the end.
    3. Select the armature, then Shift-click the mesh so it is active, and switch to Weight Paint. Ctrl-click bones to change the active group.
    4. Turn on Auto Normalize in the tool options and Zero Weights › Active in the overlays.
    5. Pose the bones to extreme positions while you paint: problems only show up in a pose. Use Blur for smooth joints.
    6. Paint one side and use X-Mirror or Weights › Mirror. Before export: Limit Total, Clean, Normalize All.
